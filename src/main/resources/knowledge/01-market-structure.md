# Market structure

## Securities and Exchange Board of India
SEBI is the regulator of the Indian securities market. It received statutory powers under the SEBI Act, 1992. SEBI's mandate is to protect investors, develop the securities market, and regulate intermediaries. Stock exchanges, brokers, mutual funds, depositories, and other market intermediaries register with SEBI and follow its regulations.

## National Stock Exchange
The National Stock Exchange of India, usually called NSE, is a leading electronic stock exchange. NSE was incorporated in 1992 and started equity trading in 1994. Its flagship index is the Nifty 50. Trading is screen-based and order-driven. NSE offers an equity cash segment, equity derivatives, currency derivatives, and debt products.

## Bombay Stock Exchange
BSE, formerly known as the Bombay Stock Exchange, was established in 1875 and is one of Asia's oldest stock exchanges. Its flagship index is the Sensex. BSE also runs an electronic, order-driven market. Many companies are listed on both NSE and BSE, so the same share can trade on either exchange. Prices on the two exchanges stay close because traders can arbitrage a gap.

## What an exchange does
A stock exchange matches buy and sell orders in listed securities. The exchange does not shop shares to investors the way a store sells goods. Prices form from supply and demand in the order book. Exchanges also run surveillance, publish prices and volumes, and set the rules of the trading session. A clearing corporation steps in as the central counterparty, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer for trades it clears. NSE Clearing clears NSE trades, and Indian Clearing Corporation clears BSE trades. That structure reduces the risk that one side of a trade fails to deliver.

## Cash market and other segments
The cash market, also called the equity segment, is where shares are bought and sold for delivery into a demat account. The derivatives segment trades futures and options, which are mostly settled in cash, except stock derivatives that can be physically settled if left open on expiry. Commodity derivatives trade mainly on MCX and NCDEX. After the Forward Markets Commission merged into SEBI in 2015, commodity derivatives also sit under SEBI. Currency derivatives such as the USD-INR contract trade on exchanges including NSE and BSE. The rupee itself is influenced by Reserve Bank of India policy, but a listed currency future is still an exchange contract with margin.
